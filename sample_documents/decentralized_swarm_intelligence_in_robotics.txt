Swarm robotics draws inspiration from social insect colonies—such as ants and bees—to coordinate hundreds of simple, autonomous robots without any centralized master controller. Instead of receiving top-down commands from a main server, individual robots rely on local sensing and decentralized interaction rules. Coordination is frequently achieved through stigmergy, a mechanism where agents communicate indirectly by modifying their shared physical environment. For example, a robot might deposit a virtual chemical marker or physical object in a specific zone, signaling nearby peers to alter their cleaning, foraging, or construction paths.

This decentralized architecture provides extreme resilience. If half a robotic swarm is disabled by a mechanical failure or physical barrier, the remaining units dynamically redistribute tasks and continue the mission without system-wide collapse. However, debugging emergent swarm behavior is notoriously difficult. Because complex collective patterns emerge from simple local interactions, minor flaws in individual agent rules can trigger unpredictable cascading failures across the entire swarm, making formal verification and safety guarantees a major hurdle for researchers.
